How AI Client Status Updates Work for Event Planners

By Ronnie Craig • November 25, 2026 • 6 min read

Every event planner knows the text. It comes in at 11pm, or on a Sunday, or while you're actually at a different client's event: "Hey, just wondering where we are on the catering?" The client isn't being difficult — they just have no way to check other than asking you directly, so they ask whenever it crosses their mind, which is constantly, because planning their event is the biggest thing on their calendar even if it's one of several on yours.

The fix isn't convincing clients to text less. It's giving them somewhere to look instead of someone to ask. Here's how that actually runs.

1. Every Completed Task Updates the Client's Status Automatically

When a vendor confirms, a deposit clears, or a timeline detail gets locked in, that task is marked complete in the system you're already tracking it in — and the client-facing status updates with it. Nobody writes a recap email summarizing progress; the status simply reflects whatever's actually done, in real time, because the update is the byproduct of the work instead of a separate task on top of it.

2. Clients Can Check Status Themselves, Any Time

Instead of a client's only option being to text you, they get a simple status view — a portal or a chat interface — where they can see what's confirmed, what's pending, and what's coming up next. The "where are we on this?" question stops needing you specifically to answer it, because the answer is sitting right there whenever they want to look.

3. Key Milestones Still Get a Proactive Update

This isn't just a dashboard clients have to remember to check. At real milestones — the venue is locked, the final headcount is due, the timeline is finalized — the client gets a message pushed to them instead of waiting to be asked. The self-serve status handles the curiosity checks; the proactive updates handle the moments that actually matter.

4. A Real Question Still Gets Routed to You

If a client asks something that needs judgment — a change request, a budget question, a problem with a vendor — that doesn't get answered by the status system. It gets flagged and routed to you, the same way it would if they'd called. The automation's job is narrow: absorb the routine "what's the status" traffic so what reaches you is the stuff that actually needs you.

5. The Update Cadence Increases as the Event Gets Closer

In the final week before the event, the proactive updates get more frequent on their own — final logistics, day-of timeline, last-minute confirmations — without you having to remember to ramp up communication during the exact week you're busiest. The system front-loads the calm weeks and tightens up automatically when it matters most.

Notice what's consistent across all five steps: automation handles status, timing, and routing. Anything that needs a planner's judgment — a decision, a problem, a change — still comes to you directly. That line doesn't move.
