The 5 Automation Flows Every Church Office Actually Needs

By Ronnie Craig • August 18, 2026 • 7 min read

When a church asks me where to start with automation, the honest answer is: not everywhere. I've seen churches try to automate twenty things at once and end up managing a pile of half-working flows nobody trusts. The churches that get real value pick two or three flows, build them right, and let staff actually feel the time coming back before adding more.

Here are the five flows that come up in almost every church I talk to, ranked by how much they actually matter — not by how impressive they sound.

1. Visitor Follow-Up (build this first)

A first-time visitor usually decides whether they're coming back before anyone on staff has had a real conversation with them. The flow: a welcome text within hours of the visit, a short personal email Monday from a real staff member, and mid-week, an invitation to one specific group instead of a generic list. The moment the visitor replies to anything, the sequence stops and a person takes over. That's the whole flow, and it's the one with the shortest decision window of anything on this list.

2. Volunteer Onboarding

Someone signs up to volunteer, and the flow confirms it immediately, sends orientation details that week, reminds them two days before their shift, and thanks them after. It also flags an unfilled shift or a volunteer who's gone quiet — the two things a busy coordinator is most likely to miss. Most churches think of this as a recruitment problem; it's almost always a follow-up problem, and this flow is the fix.

3. Event Promotion

Every church has promoted an event for three weeks and still heard "I didn't know about that" on the day. The cause usually isn't effort — it's that the announcement went out on four channels at four different times, worded four different ways, and the people who needed it weren't looking at the one they were on. This flow distributes the announcement to email, text, social, and your app at the same moment, with the reminder already scheduled for later.

4. Giving and Donation Receipts

A first-time gift is a moment worth acknowledging quickly, and a giving record is something people expect promptly at tax time. This flow triggers a same-day thank-you on a first gift and keeps receipts flowing without your finance volunteer manually exporting and emailing them one at a time. It's less emotionally loaded than the others on this list, but it's also the easiest to build and the one that quietly saves the most tedious hours.

5. Prayer Request Routing

This one is different from the rest, and deliberately so. A prayer request submitted online shouldn't sit in an inbox until someone happens to check it — the flow's job is only to route it instantly to the right person or team, with urgency flagged if the submitter marked it urgent. What the flow does not do is respond to the request itself. That reply has to come from a person. Automation here means nothing falls through the cracks; it never means the message itself gets automated.

Notice what's consistent across all five: automation handles routing, timing, and reminders. The actual words in a pastoral moment always come from a person. That line doesn't move, no matter which flow you build.

What to Build First

If you only build one flow this year, build visitor follow-up. If you build two, add volunteer onboarding. Those two consistently give back the most staff hours because they're the two most churches are currently running on memory instead of on a system — and memory is the thing most likely to fail during the exact week you need it most.

All five of these run on top of the church management software you already use — Planning Center, Breeze, Church Community Builder, Realm, Tithe.ly, Subsplash — so building one doesn't mean replacing anything.
